# 1. The World

## The realm of the Monolith

The realm is a single valley country. Sea cliffs close it to the north and west, and long escarpments cross it inside: the **Basalt Redoubt Wall**, the **Verge Wall** and the **Blight Rim**. Five holds share it. Each hold is a market town with its hamlets, villages and fields.

For three hundred years the five holds answered to one crown and one order: **the Custodians**. Each Custodian was a sworn knight-warden who guarded the roads, judged disputes between holds and, most of all, *kept the Rim*. The Custodians watched the cliff line over the old **Fracture Shelf**, where the ground split and things crawled out.

They built the **watchtower beacons** that run across the realm, so that one fire on the Rim could warn every hold by nightfall. They built the **Custodians Halls** where they judged disputes; two still stand, in Ribcage Vault and Aquifer Gate.

Their sigil was a shield before a hearth. People still call the finest gear "Custodian-grade", and the old sword-forms still carry their names (*Custodian's Riposte*, *Wall of the Custodian*), even though few people remember why.

## The Breaking (backstory, 22 years before the game)

The last king, **Aldric the Unbent**, died without an heir he had named. Within a season the lords of the five holds had pulled the Custodians apart: each hold kept "its" Custodians as house guards, and the order's shared purse, halls and oaths dissolved. Nobody manned the Rim.

Today "custodian" means a lord's household officer who keeps the granary and the books. Every keep has one. Most people have forgotten that the word once meant a sworn knight-warden. The lord's champions are now called **heroes**, three to a hold, and they serve their lord, not the realm. The beacons are still lit at dusk out of habit, but nobody remembers what they were for.

The Blight did not come at once. It came the way rot comes into grain. First the old Fracture Shelf went quiet. Then the hamlets nearest the Rim stopped sending their tithes. Then the seven rifts of the Fracture Shelf spread into what maps now call **the Necro-Plinth**, and seven walled forts along the Verge fell one by one. Folk now call that land **the Fracture Verge**.

The crown sits empty in First Monolith. No lord can take it without the other four uniting against them, and no lord trusts the other four. **Leadership is the skill of kings alone, and nobody has it.**

## The five holds

| Hold | Land | Market day | Character | What it controls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **First Monolith** | Farmland hub | Wednesday | The old royal seat. Proud, hungry, full of claimants. Its guild tavern **Fracture-Gate** faces the Fracture Verge. | Grain and the empty throne |
| **Ribcage Vault** | Woodland | Thursday | Quiet, devout and careful. Rangers and charcoal-burners. Its tavern **Occident-Needle** stands on the frontier's edge. | Timber, the best sawmills (tier 5), alchemy |
| **Aquifer Gate** | Wetland frontier | Tuesday | Poor, low-taxed and lawless. Fen-folk, fishers and smugglers. Most of the outlaw bands camp east of it. Its tavern **Oxid-Plinth** was an old Custodian outpost. | Cloth, smiths and armouries (tier 4) |
| **Sol-Spire Concourse** | Desert exchange | Monday | Rich, cosmopolitan and corrupt. It charges the highest taxes in the realm, and its oasis hamlets drink from its granary. Its tavern **Necro-Needle** looks straight at the Necro-Plinth. | Jewels (tier 5), coin and the caravan trade |
| **Basalt Redoubt** | Highlands | Friday | Hard, clannish and proud. It sits on the Basalt Redoubt Wall with its mines of black ore and sky iron. Its tavern **Cairn Gate** faces the Verge. | Metal: smelters and stoneworks (tier 5) |

Because the economy ties the holds together, they cannot fight an open war. Basalt Redoubt has steel but no grain, First Monolith has grain but no steel, and Sol-Spire has the coin to buy either. So the holds fight the *Game of Thrones* way: through tariffs, marriages, hired outlaws, poisoned guild contracts and quiet murders on the trade roads.

## The four regions

* **The Prime Basin:** the safe middle. Farms, roads and deer parks. Danger here comes from people.
* **The Spore Verge:** woods and fens on the west and east edges, with beast dens, wolves, bears and outlaw camps.
* **The Fracture Verge:** the seven fallen forts, the barrow, the deserted farm and the *horrors* that hold them.
* **The Necro-Plinth:** the old Fracture Shelf, swollen. It has 16 rifts, 22 blight nests, the calamity lairs, and **the Heart** at the centre.

## The Blight and the Heart

The Blight is not a plague; it is a *hunger*. Land it touches goes grey and salt-crusted, wells sour, and beasts born there come out wrong: **Horrors**. Where the Blight runs deepest it gathers itself into **Calamities**, beings of terrible power that each hold their own territory. Folk say a calamity "is named when it rises", because no one knows what one is until it comes.

At the centre is **the Heart**, and in the Heart sleeps a **Cataclysm**: **Drakrevor**, whom the common folk call **the Demon King**. It keeps a day of its own: it slumbers, broods and gathers power, and the Heart surges at night. The Custodians' oldest oath was written to keep Drakrevor asleep. With the Rim unwatched for twenty-two years, Drakrevor is waking. Its horde already holds every rift, every blight nest and every broken fort.

The lords know this. Each believes the Blight will hit *someone else's* hold first.
