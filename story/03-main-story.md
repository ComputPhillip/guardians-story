# 3. The Main Story: *The Guardians*

**Logline:** When the dead king's last Steward is murdered on the Riftgate road, an unknown guild recruit inherits his blade, his oath and his enemies. Then the recruit must decide whether to unite five feuding holds or take the empty throne before the Cataclysm in the Heart wakes.

The arc follows the player's progression in the systems: guild Apprentice, then Journeyman (**L20**, Awakening abilities), then Master (**L50**), then a **Titled** lord with a hall (standing 100), and finally the Heart (**L90**, Drakrevor).

---

## Act I: The Road to Riftgate (levels 1–15, Heartlands)

**Opening.** The player is a nobody: a hamlet-born recruit on their first guild contract out of **Riftgate**, escorting a grain cart to Canopy Rest. On the road, the party finds an old knight dying beside a slaughtered escort. He is **Ser Aldwin Rook**, the last Steward who still wore the oath openly. With his final breath he presses his sword into the player's hands and says:

> "Not the lords. Not the Rim. *Light the fires.*"

**What happened:** Aldwin was carrying proof that someone in the five holds has been *feeding* the Blight on purpose. The Hollow Knives killed him and took the proof, but the sword's hilt hides a fragment of the Stewards' Oath.

**Key beats**
1. Guildmaster **Odo Pell** recognises the sword and quietly takes the player under his wing, teaching them the Steward sword-forms (*Quick Slash*, *Tendon Cut*).
2. The player recruits the first companions from the Riftgate roster (up to 5 per party, 30 in all later).
3. **The Thornback Brothers** contract: Sir Robert wants the poachers hanged. The player can kill them, spare them or recruit them. This is the first moral choice, and the peasants remember it.
4. The player traces the Hollow Knives to a camp, finds a ledger paid in **Sunward coin**, and realises that a great house ordered the killing.
5. **The ford.** The **Low-Water band** holds the ford by the Chapel at the Ford and takes pennies from travellers. **Brother Roger of the Ford** has begged the lord of Canopy Rest for help. The player can clear the ford before the **Grey Company** storms it on day 5, fight beside the Company, or watch them do it. This is the player's first look at sellswords.
6. On market day in First Hearth, the player sees **Margaret the Sage awaken Robert Cordwainer**. A cobbler suddenly glows with leyline fire in front of the crowd, and word of it spreads through the inns. Magic is waking in common folk, and nobody knows why. *(The answer: Drakrevor's stirring is stirring the leylines too.)*
7. That evening the watchmen light the **beacons**, as they do every dusk. The player recognises Aldwin's last words.

**End of Act I:** Sir Robert of First Hearth summons the player to the keep, praises them and names them one of **his heroes**, with a bed at the hero lodge beside Thomas Fox. Then he asks them to deliver a sealed letter to Stonepass. *(The letter is a declaration that Sir Robert will claim the crown at the next Hearth-Moot.)*

---

## Act II: The Game of Holds (levels 15–30, Frontier)

The player travels between the five holds as a courier and contract-runner, which is how they learn the trade roads and the economy.

**Key beats**
1. **Stonepass Refuge.** Sir John Ironhand reads Sir Robert's letter and burns it. He hires the player to break the **Iron Reavers**, and the player finds out that the Reavers are paid by First Hearth. *Choice: tell Sir John Ironhand (war between the holds) or keep the secret (Sir Robert owes you).*
2. **Reedwatch.** Sir Thomas of Reedwatch is under siege from the **Grey Crows**. The player fights a defensive battle at the fords (a showcase for spear spacing and wall-pins). Wenna of Reedwatch joins as a companion.
3. **Sunward Exchange.** Sir John the Coin tries to buy the player's loyalty. Dame Matilda shows the player Blighted salt, glowing grey, being sold as an alchemical reagent. *Someone is mining the Blight.*
4. **Canopy Rest.** In the Stewards Hall, Dame Agnes of Canopy Rest shows the player a copy of the Stewards' Oath, but it is incomplete. It points to **the hidden library**. There the player learns that the Oath is not just a vow but a *ritual*: the **Rim Fires** on the Blight Rim's three lookouts, lit together by sworn Stewards, and answered by every beacon in the realm, are what kept the Demon King asleep.
5. **The Red Lances.** Robert atte Lane offers his unpaid sellswords at every keep. Hire them, and they become the player's first private army. If every lord refuses them, they turn outlaw and start squeezing Sunward's farmsteads.
6. **The stone circles.** On the same night, nine sorcerers meet in conclave at each of the five stone circles. Margaret the Sage is gathering the realm's hidden mages, and Aelis is one of them. Margaret explains that the stone circles and the beacons were built along the same leylines.
7. **Journeyman (L20).** The player's first Awakening ability unlocks, and the story frames it as the Oath *recognising* them.

**Midpoint twist: the Hearth-Moot.** All five lords meet in First Hearth to settle the crown. The moot is a trap: the Hollow Knives strike, and **Sir Thomas of Reedwatch** is murdered at the table, the realm's one honest lord. Each house blames another. The holds mobilise.

---

## Act III: The Grey Wedding (levels 30–50, the Broken March)

The realm slides toward war. To stop it, Dame Agnes of Canopy Rest proposes a marriage between **Aelis of Canopy Rest** and **Gerrit Ironhand**, which would bind woodland to highland. The wedding is set in **Ashcut Gorge**, neutral ground.

**Key beats**
1. The player clears the **Broken March** forts to reopen the road to the gorge. These are the first Horror and Elite fights and the first real Blight.
2. The player discovers that **Brother Tamsin**, Dame Agnes's confessor, is the Blight-feeder. He believes Drakrevor is a god that will "cleanse" the corrupt lords, and he has been selling Blight-salt through Sunward.
3. **The Grey Wedding.** At the feast, Tamsin breaks a sealed jar of Blight-salt. Horrors pour from the gorge walls, and the Iron Reavers (bought by Sir Robert) strike in the chaos. **Thomas Fox** faces the player in a duel under the gorge switchbacks.
   * *Choice:* Kill Thomas Fox, or spare him and learn that Sir Robert ordered the Reavers in but knew nothing of Tamsin.
4. Gerrit Ironhand dies holding the switchback (the *Lock Shields* scene). Whether Aelis lives depends on the player's positioning in the battle, a real fail state with permanent consequences.
5. **Master (L50).** The player masters the Steward blade. *Steward's Riposte* is unlocked.

**End of Act III:** The first **Calamity** rises from a lair 9 km from the Heart, and the Blight Rim's lookouts go dark. Drakrevor is stirring.

---

## Act IV: The Unlit Fires (levels 50–75, the Blighted's edge)

The holds are now at open war. Stonepass blames First Hearth for Gerrit's death, Sunward profits from both sides, and Reedwatch has fallen to Harlan Grey's Crows.

**Key beats**
1. **A title is offered.** Whichever house the player has helped most offers them a hall and a title (*Titled*, standing 100). The player builds their **Hall**, and from now on recruits, staff, income and defence matter to the story. The player now *has* a seat at the table.
2. **The Siege of the Stonepass Wall.** First Hearth marches on Stonepass. The player chooses a side, or holds the passes for neither and turns both armies toward the Rim.
3. **The free calamities.** The two northern calamities (15 km and 16 km from the Heart) are Drakrevor's rivals, *neutral* to the horde. Dame Matilda proposes using one against the other. This is a dangerous pact that can win the war or lose a hold.
4. **Reforging the Stewards.** The player gathers companions who have taken the Oath: Wenna, Aelis (if alive), Old Torbin, Odo Pell, a redeemed Thomas Fox and others. The Oath needs **three sworn Stewards, one at each Rim lookout**, to light the fires at the same moment.
5. **The Rim Fires.** A three-front battle at the three Blight Rim lookouts, each held by a companion squad while the player moves between them. The player lights the fires, and Drakrevor's waking slows but does not stop. The fires buy weeks, not years.

**End of Act IV:** Tamsin flees into the Heart. The only lasting answer is to go in.

---

## Act V: The Heart (levels 75–90+, the Heart)

**Key beats**
1. **The last Hearth-Moot.** The surviving lords meet one final time. The player must convince them to send their garrisons to the Blighted. What is possible here depends on every earlier choice: which houses trust the player, which lords are alive, and who holds Reedwatch.
2. **The march to the Heart** passes rifts, blight nests and calamity lairs. Companions can fall permanently.
3. **Drakrevor** (L90, tier 5, 15% True damage) is a boss that punishes a stationary party, the purest test of the positioning system. A *Balanced Guild* party with T5 elixirs is the "intended" winning build.

## Endings

The ending depends on what the player does with the crown after the Heart:

| Ending | Condition | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **The Steward's Watch** | Refuse the throne and re-found the Stewards | The holds stay divided but bound by the Oath. The Rim is manned forever, the player becomes the first Lord-Steward, and the throne stays empty. *(The bittersweet "true" ending.)* |
| **The Hearth King** | Take the throne with at least 3 houses' support | The realm is united under the player. Leadership, the skill of kings alone, unlocks. Drakrevor is sealed, but the defeated houses plot. *(A sequel hook.)* |
| **The Puppet Crown** | Put a lord on the throne (Sir Robert, Sunward or Durn) | Peace that lasts as long as the puppet does. The epilogue varies by lord. |
| **The Grey Peace** | Make a pact with a free calamity | The war is won quickly. Years later, the calamity "is named" and rises against the realm. *(The dark ending.)* |
| **The Fall of the Hearth** | Fail at the Heart, or lose more than 3 holds | Drakrevor wakes, and the epilogue shows the Blight reaching First Hearth. |
